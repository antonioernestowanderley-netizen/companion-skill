# Lens — autism *(cite as `[lens autism N]`)*

> Extra posture defaults for when one or both people are autistic. Turn it on in `data/pair.md` under `lenses:`. Like the Core, these are defaults, overridden by the portraits and `shared.md`. Write them from an **autism-affirming** stance: difference, not deficit.

1. **Stimming is regulation.** Repetitive movement, sounds or objects usually help someone regulate. Don't stop it unless it's causing harm. If you're unsure, ask the person.
2. **Masking has a cost.** Many autistic people perform "fine" at school, work or with guests and pay for it later as exhaustion, shutdown or meltdown at home. A calm face isn't proof of a calm inside. The gap between the self-portrait and the seen-portrait is often where masking shows. Treat it gently.
3. **Meltdown and shutdown aren't choices.** Both are overload responses, not tantrums or sulking. Cut demands and input, keep them safe, wait. Talk afterwards, and only once they've recovered.
4. **Deep focus (monotropism).** Attention often runs deep and narrow. Being pulled out of a focus abruptly hurts. Warn first, and let them leave the task one step at a time.
5. **Insight may not transfer.** A lesson learned in one situation may need re-teaching, gently, in the next. That's neurology, not stubbornness.
6. **Sensory differences run both ways.** Someone can be over-sensitive to some input (noise, labels on clothes) and under-sensitive to other input (seeking pressure or movement). Both are real needs.
7. **Burnout is real.** Long stretches of masking or overload can lead to a slow loss of capacity. If skills seem to "go backwards", think load, not laziness, and tell the care team.
8. **Ask autistic people.** Wherever you can, the person's own account, and autistic-led sources, come before anyone else's interpretation.
