# Translation guide — from how we ask to how they think

> The heart of this skill. Most everyday questions are built for people who summarise, read between the lines, and name feelings on demand. Many autistic people think in **specifics**: what happened, who was there, what it felt like to the senses. This guide turns one into the other.
> It is **posture, not prescription**. The person's **profile and Question bank always win**. What has actually worked for *them* beats every example here.

## Why "How was your day?" fails

It sounds simple, but it asks for five hard things at once:

| Hidden demand | Why it's hard |
|---|---|
| **Summarise** a whole day | No anchor. Where do you even start? Six hours, hundreds of moments. |
| **Evaluate** it (good/bad) | It's abstract, and the day was probably a mix. |
| **Name a feeling** | Many autistic people find it hard to identify and describe their own emotions (*alexithymia*: about half of the autistic adults studied, per Kinnaird et al., 2019). It's not that the feeling isn't there. The label is hard to reach. |
| **Play the social ritual** | "How was your day?" is often a ritual, not a real question. Many autistic people have learned that "fine" ends it, so you get the script, not the day (masking: Hull et al., 2017). |
| **Answer now** | Processing an open question takes time, and the gap gets filled with another question. |

So you get "fine", "I don't know", silence, or a meltdown later that seems to "come from nowhere". None of those mean they don't want to connect. **The question didn't fit.**

## The method: five moves

1. **Anchor in one specific, real thing.** A person, a place, a time, an event they know about. Draw it from *Their world* in the profile.
   "How was school?" → "Did **Leo** come to class today?"
2. **Make it answerable.** Yes/no, this-or-that, a name, a number. Never "how" or "why" first.
   "What did you do?" → "Did you have **art or PE** today?"
3. **One question. Then wait.** Wait longer than feels natural (the profile says how long). Silence is processing, not refusal.
4. **Go from outside to inside.** Facts first, feelings last, through the ladder below.
5. **Follow their answer, not your script.** The next question builds on what they gave you. Two or three questions is a conversation. Ten is an interrogation. Stop while it's still good.

### The ladder: reaching a feeling through facts
Climb only as far as they want to go. Many good conversations stop at step 2, and that's still connection.

| Step | Ask about | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Facts** | what happened, who, where | "Did Leo sit next to you at lunch?" |
| 2. **Senses** | sound, light, temperature, crowd | "Was the classroom loud or quiet?" · "Was the room cold?" |
| 3. **Body** | tired, hungry, sore, energy | "Is your body tired or full of energy?" · "Does your tummy feel OK?" *(Body signals can be hard to read too; name a body part.)* |
| 4. **Rating** | their scale (profile: 1–5, colours, emoji, thumbs) | "Good day, OK day, or hard day? Pick one." |
| 5. **Feeling words** | only if they use them | "When the fire alarm went off: scary, loud, or both?" |

## Translations

> Names in **bold** are examples; the companion fills them from the profile. If the profile doesn't have them, it says so, and that's a sign to add them.

### Asking how they feel (the reason for all of this)
| You'd naturally say | Try instead (one at a time) |
|---|---|
| "How do you feel?" | "Is your body tired or full of energy?" → "Is it loud in here for you?" → "Pick one: 😀 😐 😣" *(their scale)* |
| "Are you sad?" | "Did something happen today that you didn't like? Yes or no." → "Was it at school or at home?" |
| "Are you happy at your new school?" | "Who do you sit with at lunch?" → "Is the classroom loud or quiet?" → "Old school or new school: which is better?" |
| "Are you worried about tomorrow?" | "Tomorrow is the dentist at 10. Do you want to see a picture of the room?" → "Is there one thing about it you don't like?" |
| "Did you have a good time?" | "What was the best part: the cake or the games?" → "Good, OK, or hard? Pick one." |

### Asking about their day / an event
| You'd naturally say | Try instead (one at a time) |
|---|---|
| "How was your day?" | "Did **Leo** come to school today?" → "Was the classroom loud or quiet?" → "Good day, OK day, or hard day?" |
| "How was school?" | "What did you have for lunch?" · "Did **Ms Rivera** teach today, or a substitute?" |
| "Did you have fun at the party?" | "Who was at the party?" → "Was the music loud?" → "Next party: go or skip?" |
| "Tell me about your day" | "Tell me **one** thing from science class." |
| "Anything happen today?" | "Did anything **different** happen today? Yes or no." |

### When something seems wrong
*Regulate first (Core 3). These are for after, when they're calm.*
| You'd naturally say | Try instead |
|---|---|
| "What's wrong?" | "Is something hurting? Yes or no." · "Is it too loud here?" |
| "Are you OK?" | "Do you want quiet, or do you want me to stay?" |
| "Why did you do that?" | Skip *why*. Ask "What happened just before?" or "Was it too loud?" |
| "Did someone upset you?" | "Did something change today that you didn't expect?" |
| "Calm down." | (Say nothing; lower the input.) Later: "Do you want the headphones?" |

### Plans, instructions, uncertainty
| You'd naturally say | Try instead |
|---|---|
| "We'll see." / "Maybe later." | "Not today. Saturday, yes." · "I don't know yet. I'll tell you at dinner." |
| "Hurry up!" | "Shoes on now. We leave in 5 minutes." |
| "Be good at grandma's." | "At grandma's: say hello, eat dinner, then tablet." |
| "Are you nervous about the trip?" | "Tomorrow we fly at 9. Want to see pictures of the hotel?" → "Bring **the blue dinosaur** or **the book**?" |
| "In a minute." | "After this song." · "When the timer rings." |

### With teens and adults
| You'd naturally say | Try instead |
|---|---|
| "How are you?" | "Is today a talking day or a quiet day?" |
| "Do you want to talk about it?" | "Talk now, later in the car, or by text?" |
| "How was work?" | "Did the 10 o'clock meeting happen?" → "Was the office busy or calm?" |
| "Are you upset with me?" | "Did I do something today that bothered you? You can text me." |

### Em português: traduza a expressão, não só as palavras
Everyday Portuguese has its own vague idioms. Translate those, not just the words.
| Você diria | Tente |
|---|---|
| "Como foi seu dia?" | "O **Leo** foi à aula hoje?" → "A sala estava barulhenta ou quieta?" → "Dia bom, mais ou menos, ou difícil?" |
| "Tudo bem?" | "Seu corpo está cansado ou com energia?" |
| "Daqui a pouco." | "Depois do jantar." · "Quando o alarme tocar." |
| "Vamos ver." | "Hoje não. Sábado, sim." · "Ainda não sei. Te falo no jantar." |
| "Se comporta na casa da vovó." | "Na casa da vovó: dar oi, jantar, depois tablet." |
| "O que foi?" | *(Primeiro acalmar.)* Depois: "Está doendo alguma coisa? Sim ou não." |

## Reading the answer back
- **A literal answer is a true answer.** "It was a day." is accurate. Ask a more specific question; don't correct the answer.
- **Short isn't rude.** "Yes." is a complete reply. Build the next question on it.
- **"I don't know" often means "I can't find the words"**, not "I won't tell you". Offer two choices.
- **Silence means wait.** Count it out (the profile says how long). Don't rephrase too soon; a new question restarts the processing.
- **One answer is a moment; a pattern takes many.** The ledger holds the moments. The companion only calls something a pattern after it has seen it several times.
- **Never turn an answer into a diagnosis.** "The room was cold" is information, not a symptom.

## Timing and channel matter as much as words
- **Decompression first.** Straight after school, many people need quiet before any questions. The profile says how long.
- **Side by side beats face to face.** In the car, walking, cooking, during their interest. Less eye contact means less load.
- **Writing can beat speaking.** A text, a notebook, a drawing, a choice board.
- **Through their interest.** "If today were a train journey, was it on time or delayed?" works brilliantly for some people and not at all for others. The profile decides.
