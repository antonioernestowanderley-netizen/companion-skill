# Bibliography — the north star

> Every principle in `common-core.md` and `translation-guide.md` should trace back to something here. **If the guide and these sources disagree, the sources win and the guide gets fixed.** If the sources and an autistic person's own account disagree, **the person wins.** This list prefers autistic voices and peer-reviewed work. It is a reading list for the people who use and improve this skill, not clinical advice.

## 1. Misunderstanding is mutual — the stance behind everything
- **Milton, D. E. M. (2012).** On the ontological status of autism: the "double empathy problem". *Disability & Society*, 27(6), 883–887.
  → Breakdowns happen *between* two ways of thinking, not inside the autistic person. That's why the owner's question gets translated, rather than the child being asked to answer "better".
- **Crompton, C. J., Ropar, D., Evans-Williams, C. V. M., Flynn, E. G., & Fletcher-Watson, S. (2020).** Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective. *Autism*, 24(7), 1704–1712.
  → Evidence for double empathy: information passed along chains of autistic people as well as along chains of non-autistic people, and broke down mainly in mixed chains. The mismatch is the problem.
- **Mitchell, P., Sheppard, E., & Cassidy, S. (2021).** Autism and the double empathy problem: Implications for development and mental health. *British Journal of Developmental Psychology*, 39(1), 1–18.

## 2. Why "How do you feel?" is hard — the ladder
- **Kinnaird, E., Stewart, C., & Tchanturia, K. (2019).** Investigating alexithymia in autism: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *European Psychiatry*, 55, 80–89.
  → About half of the autistic adults studied had marked difficulty identifying and describing their emotions (around 5% of comparison groups). That's why feelings are reached through facts, senses and body first.
- **Bird, G., & Cook, R. (2013).** Mixed emotions: the contribution of alexithymia to the emotional symptoms of autism. *Translational Psychiatry*, 3, e285.
- **Mazefsky, C. A., Herrington, J., Siegel, M., Scarpa, A., Maddox, B. B., Scahill, L., & White, S. W. (2013).** The role of emotion regulation in autism spectrum disorder. *Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry*, 52(7), 679–688.
  → Regulation before questions (Core 3, Rule 5).
- **Garfinkel, S. N., Tiley, C., O'Keeffe, S., Harrison, N. A., Seth, A. K., & Critchley, H. D. (2016).** Discrepancies between dimensions of interoception in autism: Implications for emotion and anxiety. *Biological Psychology*, 114, 117–126.
  → Reading your own body's signals can differ in autism too. That's why the body step names a body part ("Does your tummy feel OK?") rather than asking "How does your body feel?".
- **Hull, L., Petrides, K. V., Allison, C., Smith, P., Baron-Cohen, S., Lai, M.-C., & Mandy, W. (2017).** "Putting on my best normal": Social camouflaging in adults with autism spectrum conditions. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, 47(8), 2519–2534.
  → Camouflaging is effortful and exhausting. "Fine" can be the mask, and home is where it comes off: decompression before questions.

## 3. Literal, specific, one thing at a time — how the method works
- **Vermeulen, P. (2001).** *Autistic Thinking: This Is the Title.* Jessica Kingsley.
  → Literal and concrete thinking from the inside of the logic. The direct source of "make it specific, make it answerable".
- **Vermeulen, P. (2012).** *Autism as Context Blindness.* AAPC Publishing.
  → Open questions rely on context the listener has to fill in. Anchoring in a named person, place or event supplies that context.
- **Murray, D., Lesser, M., & Lawson, W. (2005).** Attention, monotropism and the diagnostic criteria for autism. *Autism*, 9(2), 139–156.
  → Deep, narrow attention: one question at a time, warnings before switching, interests as the bridge.
- **Robertson, C. E., & Baron-Cohen, S. (2017).** Sensory perception in autism. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 18(11), 671–684.
  → Sensory experience is central to autism, not peripheral. That's why the ladder asks about the room (loud? cold? bright?) before asking about feelings.

## 4. Autistic voices — read these first
- **Higashida, N. (2013).** *The Reason I Jump* (trans. K. A. Yoshida & D. Mitchell). Sceptre.
  → A non-speaking teenager on what questions feel like from the inside, and why the answer takes time.
- **Grandin, T. (1995; expanded 2006).** *Thinking in Pictures.* Vintage.
  → Concrete, visual thinking. The source for "show, don't only tell" and visual supports.
- **Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN)**, autisticadvocacy.org. Autistic-led guidance; "nothing about us without us".
- **Shore, S.**, the source of "If you've met one person with autism, you've met one person with autism." That's the golden rule of the Core.

## 5. Practice frameworks — how professionals do it
- **Prizant, B. M., with Fields-Meyer, T. (2015).** *Uniquely Human: A Different Way of Seeing Autism.* Simon & Schuster.
  → Behaviours are attempts to cope and to communicate: ask *why* before trying to stop them (Core 2). Regulation first, strengths first.
- **Prizant, B. M., Wetherby, A. M., Rubin, E., Laurent, A. C., & Rydell, P. J. (2006).** *The SCERTS Model.* Paul H. Brookes.
  → Social communication, emotional regulation and transactional support. The adult adapts, not only the child.
- **Hume, K., Steinbrenner, J. R., Odom, S. L., et al. (2021).** Evidence-based practices for children, youth, and young adults with autism: Third generation review. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, 51, 4013–4032.
  → Visual supports, prompting and antecedent-based strategies (preparing someone before a change) are among the established practices. They back "show me" options, "first/then" and warnings before change.
- **Gray, C. (2015).** *The New Social Story Book* (15th anniversary ed.). Future Horizons.
  → Concrete, literal descriptions of situations. The model for translating plans and instructions.
- **Fletcher-Watson, S., & Happé, F. (2019).** *Autism: A New Introduction to Psychological Theory and Current Debate.* Routledge.
  → A balanced map of current science, including the neurodiversity perspective.

## 6. Organisations with practical communication guidance
- **National Autistic Society (UK)**, autism.org.uk. Communication tips for families.
- **Autistica**, autistica.org.uk. Research charity; plain-language summaries.

### How this list grows
New sources are added the same way the Core grows: reviewed before they land, each one linked to the principle it supports, and autistic-authored and peer-reviewed work preferred. If you know a source that would sharpen or correct something here, open an issue.
